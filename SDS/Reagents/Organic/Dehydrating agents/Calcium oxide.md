### Description

- Calcium oxide appears as an odorless, white or gray-white solid in the form of hard lumps. A strong irritant to skin, eyes and mucous membranes. Used in insecticides and fertilizers.[^1]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

- Readily absorbs carbon dioxide and [[Water|water]] from air, becoming airslaked.[^4]

### Hazardous decomposition

- Decomposition product: calcium hydroxide.[^4]

### Corrosivity

- Corrosive[^4]

### Chemical dangers

- The solution in water is a medium strong base. Reacts with water. This generates sufficient heat to ignite combustible materials. Reacts violently with acids, halogens and metals.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Crumbles on exposure to moist air. Reacts with water to form corrosive calcium hydroxide, with evolution of much heat. Temperatures as high as 800 °C have been reached with addition of water (moisture in air or soil). The heat of this reaction has caused ignition of neighboring quantities of sulfur, gunpowder, wood, and straw \[Mellor 3: 673 1946-47\].[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Bases, Strong[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- A base and an oxidizing agent. Neutralizes acids with generation of heat. Nonflammable, but will support combustion by liberation of oxygen, especially in the presence of organic materials. Reacts very violently with liquid hydrofluoric acid \[Mellor 2, Supp. 1:129 1956\]. Reacts extremely violently with phosphorus pentaoxide when reaction is initiated by local heating \[Mellor 8 Supp.3:406 1971\].[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Water (liberates heat), [[Fluorine|fluorine]], ethanol \[Note: Reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide.\][^13]
- Addn of water to quicklime has generated temp as high as 800 °C. Some reports describe reaction as violent. Ignition of sulfur, gunpowder, wood, and straw by heat of quicklime-water reaction has been reported.[^4]
- Even when cold, fluorine will attack calcium oxide, evolving much heat and some light.[^4]
- [[Chlorine trifluoride]] reacts violently, producing flame, with...calcium oxide.[^4]

## 11. Toxicological Information

### Inhalation risk

- Evaporation at 20 °C is negligible; a harmful concentration of airborne particles can, however, be reached quickly when dispersed.[^2]

### Short-term exposure

- The substance is corrosive to the eyes, skin and respiratory tract. The effects may be delayed. Medical observation is indicated.[^2]

### Long-term exposure

- Repeated or prolonged contact with skin may cause dermatitis. Repeated or prolonged inhalation of dust particles may cause effects on the lungs. Repeated or prolonged inhalation may cause nasal ulceration. This may result in perforation of the nasal septum.[^2]

### Routes of exposure

- The substance can be absorbed into the body by inhalation of its aerosol and by ingestion.[^2]
- inhalation, ingestion, skin and/or eye contact[^13]

### Signs and symptoms

- **Inhalation Exposure:** Burning sensation. Cough. Shortness of breath. Sore throat.[^2]
- **Skin Exposure:** Dry skin. Redness. Skin burns. Burning sensation. Pain.[^2]
- **Eye Exposure:** Redness. Pain. Blurred vision. Severe deep burns.[^2]

### Target organs

- Eyes, skin, respiratory system[^13]

### Human toxicity

- /SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS/ Industrial experience has shown calcium oxide to be very irritating to mucous membranes and moist skin as a result of local liberation of heat and dehydration of tissues upon slaking of the small size particles and the resulting alkalinity of the slaked product. Inflammation of respiratory passages and ulceration and perforation of the nasal septum have been attributed to inhalation of lime dust. Major complaints of workers exposed to lime consist of eye and skin irritation.[^4]
- /SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS/ Calcium oxide dust irritates the eyes and upper respiratory tract primarily because of its alkalinity. Inflammation of the respiratory passages, ulceration and perforation of the nasal septum, and pneumonia have been attributed to inhalation of calcium oxide dust.[^4]
