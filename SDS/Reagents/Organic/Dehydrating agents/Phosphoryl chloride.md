### Description

- Phosphorus oxychloride appears as a colorless fuming liquid with a pungent odor. Density 14.0 lb / gal. Very toxic by inhalation and corrosive to metals and tissue. Used in gasoline additives and hydraulic fluids.[^1]
- BROWN FUMING LIQUID WITH PUNGENT ODOUR.[^2]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Hazardous decomposition

- WHEN HEATED TO DECOMPOSITION, IT EMITS HIGHLY TOXIC FUMES OF CHLORIDES AND PO(X).[^4]
- PRODUCES PHOSPHORUS TRICHLORIDE & CHLORINE WHEN HEATED; PRODUCES PHOSPHORUS OXYCHLORIDE, PHOSPHORIC ACID, & [[Hydrochloric acid (32-38%)|HYDROCHLORIC ACID]] WHEN DECOMPOSED IN [[Water|WATER]][^4]

### Corrosivity

- PRODUCTS OF ITS REACTION WITH WATER RAPIDLY CORRODE STEEL & MOST METALS[^4]

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes on heating. This produces toxic and corrosive fumes including hydrogen chloride and phosphorus oxides. Reacts violently with water. This produces heat and decomposition products including hydrochloric acid and phosphoric acid. This generates fire and explosion hazard. Reacts violently with alcohols, phenols, amines and many other materials.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Fumes in air. Reacts exothermically (with possible spattering) with water to form fumes of phosphoric acid and hydrochloric acid. Phosphorus oxychloride reacts vigorously with water to generate gaseous HCl. Based on a scenario where the chemical is spilled into an excess of water (at least 5 fold excess of water), half of the maximum theoretical yield of Hydrogen Chloride (hydrochloric acid) gas will be created in 0.12 minutes. Experimental details are in the following: "Development of the Table of Initial Isolation and Protective Distances for the 2008 Emergency Response Guidebook", ANL/DIS-09-2, D.F. Brown, H.M. Hartmann, W.A. Freeman, and W.D. …[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Acyl Halides, Sulfonyl Halides, and Chloroformates[^1]
