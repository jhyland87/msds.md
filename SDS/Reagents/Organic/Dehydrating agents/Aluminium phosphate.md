### Description

- Aluminum phosphate, solution appears as a colorless liquid. Insoluble in [[Water|water]]. Corrosive to metals and tissue.[^1]
- WHITE CRYSTALLINE POWDER.[^2]

### Recommended use and restrictions

- **Sources/Uses:** Naturally occurring in the minerals angelite, coeruleolactite, evansite, lucinite, metavariscite, sterretite, variscite, vashegyite, wavellite, and zepharovicht; Used as cement in admixture with calcium sulfate and sodium silicate, as flux for ceramics, in dental cements, for special glasses, and as therapeutic antacid; \[Merck Index\][^3]
- **Industrial Processes with risk of exposure:** Glass Manufacturing \[Category: Industry\][^3]
- **Activities with risk of exposure:** Ceramics making \[Category: Hobbies\] Enameling \[Category: Hobbies\][^3]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes on heating at high temperatures. This produces toxic fumes including phosphorus oxides. Reacts violently with strong acids and strong bases.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Insoluble in [[Water|water]].[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Salts, Acidic Water and Aqueous Solutions[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- An acid. The resulting aqueous solutions contain moderate concentrations of hydrogen ions and have pH's of less than 7.0. They react as acids to neutralize bases. These neutralizations generate heat, but less or far less than is generated by neutralization of inorganic acids, inorganic oxoacids, and carboxylic acid. They usually do not react as either oxidizing agents or reducing agents but such behavior is not impossible. Many of these compounds catalyze organic reactions.[^1]
