## 7. Handling and Storage

### Precautions for safe handling

- ... HANDLING OF PYRIDINE SHOULD BE CONDUCTED IN WELL-VENTILATED CONDITIONS ... .[^8]
- Open lights and other /ignition sources/ capable of igniting pyridine and its vapor should be excluded from areas where pyridine is exposed in the course of manipulation.[^8]
- Employees should wash immediately when skin is wet or contaminated. Remove clothing immediately if wet or contaminated to avoid flammability hazard. Provide emergency showers and eyewash.[^8]

### Safe storage

- Store only in original container. Fireproof. Separated from strong oxidants and strong acids. Cool. Dry. Well closed. Store in an area without drain or sewer access.[^2]

### Storage conditions

- OUTSIDE OR DETACHED STORAGE IS PREFERABLE. ISOLATE FROM POWERFUL OXIDIZING MATERIALS AND ACIDS.[^8]
- Mixtures with formamide + iodine + sulfur trioxide are storage hazards, releasing carbon dioxide & [[Sulfuric acid|sulfuric acid]].[^8]

### Fire prevention

- NO open flames, NO sparks and NO smoking. Closed system, ventilation, explosion-proof electrical equipment and lighting. Do NOT use compressed air for filling, discharging, or handling.[^2]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

- Mixtures with formamide + iodine + sulfur trioxide are storage hazards, releasing carbon dioxide & [[Sulfuric acid|sulfuric acid]].[^8]

### Hazardous decomposition

- When pyridine is heated to decomposition, cyanide fumes are released.[^8]
- Pyridine causes maleic anhydride to decompose exothermally.[^8]

### Corrosivity

- Liquid pyridine will attack some forms of plastics, rubber and coatings.[^8]

### Polymerization

- Forms highly explosive by product with [[Trifluoromethyl hypofluorite|trifluoromethyl hypofluorite]] in reactions where used as an acid-acceptor.[^8]

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes on burning. This produces toxic fumes of nitrogen oxides and hydrogen cyanide. Reacts violently with strong oxidants and strong acids. This generates fire and explosion hazard. The substance is a weak base.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Highly flammable. Soluble in [[Water|water]].[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Amines, Phosphines, and Pyridines[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- PYRIDINE is a base. Reacts exothermically with acids. During preparation of a complex of pyridine with chromium trioxide, an acid, the proportion of chromium trioxide was increased. Heating from this acid-base reaction led to an explosion and fire \[MCA Case History 1284 1967\]. A 0.1% solution of pyridine (or other tertiary amine) in maleic anhydride at 185 °C gives an exothermic decomposition with rapid evolution of gas \[Chem Eng. News 42(8); 41 1964\]. …[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Strong oxidizers, strong acids[^13]
- PYRIDINE REACTS VIOLENTLY WITH CHLOROSULFONIC ACID, CHROMIC ACID, MALEIC ANHYDRIDE, [[Nitric acid (azeotropic, 68%)|NITRIC ACID]], FUMING SULFURIC ACID, PERCHROMATES, BETA-PROPIOLACTONE, SILVER PERCHLORATE, & SULFURIC ACID.[^8]
- Incompatible with strong oxidizers.[^8]
- ... Contact with strong acids will cause violent spattering.[^8]

## 11. Toxicological Information

### Inhalation risk

- A harmful contamination of the air can be reached rather quickly on evaporation of this substance at 20 °C.[^2]

### Short-term exposure

- The substance is irritating to the eyes, skin and respiratory tract. The substance may cause effects on the central nervous system. Exposure far above the OEL could cause lowering of consciousness.[^2]

### Long-term exposure

- This substance is possibly carcinogenic to humans.[^2]

### Carcinogenicity

- A3: Confirmed animal carcinogen with unknown relevance to humans.[^8]
- **IARC Carcinogenic Agent:** Pyridine[^17]
- **IARC Carcinogenic Classes:** Group 2B: Possibly carcinogenic to humans[^17]
- **IARC Monographs:** Volume 77: (2000) Some Industrial Chemicals Volume 119: (2019) Some Chemicals That Cause Tumours of the Urinary Tract in Rodents[^17]

### Routes of exposure

- The substance can be absorbed into the body by inhalation, through the skin and by ingestion.[^2]
- inhalation, skin absorption, ingestion, skin and/or eye contact[^13]

### Signs and symptoms

- **Inhalation Exposure:** Cough. Dizziness. Headache. Nausea. Shortness of breath. Unconsciousness.[^2]
- **Skin Exposure:** MAY BE ABSORBED! Redness. Burning sensation. Further see Inhalation.[^2]
- **Eye Exposure:** Redness. Pain.[^2]

### Target organs

- Eyes, skin, central nervous system, liver, kidneys, gastrointestinal tract[^13]
