## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Air and water reactions

- No rapid reaction with air. No rapid reaction with [[Water|water]].[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Amines, Phosphines, and Pyridines Acyl Halides, Sulfonyl Halides, and Chloroformates Fluoride Salts, Soluble[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- Cyanuric fluoride is used as a mild fluorinating agent for organic chemicals, and it reacts with water in a manner similar to an acyl halide, forming hydrofluoric acid and cyanuric acid. Since it forms hydrofluoric acid, cyanuric fluoride is a source of soluble fluoride ions. Unlike other halide ions, fluoride is quite reactive, acting as a weak base and participating in some unique reactions. In particular, fluorides react strongly with compounds containing calcium, magnesium, or silicon ions, which means that solutions containing soluble fluorides are corrosive to both living tissue and glass. …[^1]
