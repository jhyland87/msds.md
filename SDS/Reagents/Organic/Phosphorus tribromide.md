### Description

- Phosphorus tribromide appears as a colorless fuming liquid with a pungent odor. Corrosive to metals and tissue. Boiling point 347 °F (175 °C). Freezing point -40 °F (-40 °C).[^1]
- Colorless to yellow liquid with a very penetrating odor; \[HSDB\][^2]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Hazardous decomposition

- ... If involved in a fire, /phosphorus tribromide/ decomposes to produce hydrogen bromide, phosphoric acid, phosphine.[^3]
- When heated to decomposition it emits toxic fumes of /hydrogen bromide and phosphorous oxides/.[^3]

### Corrosivity

- IN PRESENCE OF MOISTURE, HIGHLY CORROSIVE TO MOST METALS EXCEPT LEAD & NICKEL[^3]

### Air and water reactions

- Fumes in air. Decomposed by [[Water|water]] to form phosphorous acid and hydrobromic acid. Reaction with warm water is very rapid and may be violent \[Mellor v.8. 1032 1940\]. Based on a scenario where the chemical is spilled into an excess of water (at least 5 fold excess of water), half of the maximum theoretical yield of Hydrogen Bromide gas will be created in 11 minutes. Experimental details are in the following: "Development of the Table of Initial Isolation and Protective Distances for the 2008 Emergency Response Guidebook", ANL/DIS-09-2, D.F. Brown, H.M. Hartmann, W.A. Freeman, and W.D. Haney, Argonne National Laboratory, Argonne, Illinois, June 2009.[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Acids, Strong Non-oxidizing Reducing Agents, Strong[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- PHOSPHORUS TRIBROMIDE reacts with oxidizing agents to generate heat and products that may be flammable, combustible, or otherwise reactive; the reactions may be violent. Forms complexes with potassium or sodium metal that explode when shocked. Dropwise addition to 3-phenylpropanol caused an explosion when stirring of the mixture was discontinued \[Chem. Brit., 1974, 10, 101-102\].[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Will react with water, steam or acids to produce heat, toxic and corrosive fumes.[^3]
- Reacts violently with water, evolving hydrogen bromide, an irritating & corrosive gas apparent as white fumes.[^3]
- Interaction with warm water is very rapid and may be violent with limited quantities.[^3]
- During disposal of the tribromide by a recommended procedure involving slow addition of a mixture of soda ask (sodium carbonate) and dry slaked lime (calcium hydroxide), a violent reaction, accompanied by flame, occurred a few sec after the first drop. Cautious addition of the bromide to a large volume of ice water is suggested for disposal.[^3]
