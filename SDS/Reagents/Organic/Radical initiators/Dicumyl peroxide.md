## 5. Fire-Fighting Measures

### Explosion hazards

- Severe explosion hazard when shocked, exposed to heat, or by spontaneous chemical reaction. /Organic peroxides/[^4]
- Seven organic peroxides were tested for propagation and power of explosion, and for shock sensitivity. ## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

- Stable under recommended storage conditions.[^4]

### Hazardous decomposition

- 130 °C[^2]
- The substance decomposes rapidly on heating and under influence of light, causing fire and explosion hazard.[^4]

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes rapidly on heating. Decomposes rapidly under the influence of light. This generates fire and explosion hazard. Reacts violently with acids, bases, reducing agents and heavy metals.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- No rapid reaction with air. No rapid reaction with [[Water|water]].[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Peroxides, Organic[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- The explosive instability of the lower dialkyl peroxides (e.g., dimethyl peroxide) and 1,1-bis-peroxides decreases rapidly with increasing chain length and degree of branching, the di-tert-alkyl derivatives being amongst the most stable class of peroxides. Though many 1,1-bis-peroxides have been reported, few have been purified because of the higher explosion hazards compared with the monofunctional peroxides. It is unlikely that this derivative would be particularly unstable compared to other peroxides in its class \[Bretherick 2nd ed., p 44 1979\].[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Reacts violently with acids, bases, reducing agents, and heavy metals.[^4]
- Upon contact with reducing materials, such as organic matter or thiocyanates, an explosion can occur. /Organic peroxides/[^4]

### Toxicity summary

- IDENTIFICATION AND USE: Dicumyl peroxide is a pale yellow to white granular solid. It is used as a polymerization catalyst and vulcanizing agent. HUMAN STUDIES: Organic peroxides in contact with skin can cause irritation and even burns. A case of occupational asthma related to dicumyl peroxide has been reported. Otorhinolaryngologic examination revealed upper respiratory tract diseases in 33.3% of the workers exposed to dicumyl peroxide. ANIMAL STUDIES: In the most severe injuries induced in rabbit eyes there has been great chemosis and formation of gas bubbles in the conjunctiva, cornea, and anterior chamber. Conjunctiva has become necrotic and the cornea opaque. …[^4]

### Human toxicity

- /SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS/ Organic peroxides in contact with skin can cause irritation and even burns. /Organic peroxides/[^4]
- /CASE REPORTS/ We report the case of an electrical cable repairer who presented with symptoms suggestive of occupational asthma. A supervised workplace challenge test confirmed this diagnosis and laboratory challenge studies implicated heated polyethylene repair tape containing the chemical cross-linking agent dicumyl peroxide as the cause. Similar cross-linking processes are widely used in the cable manufacturing industry and the possibility of occupational asthma occurring in other settings may need to be considered.[^4]

## 12. Ecological Information

*Non-mandatory for OSHA enforcement (29 CFR 1910.1200).*

### Environmental hazards

- The substance is toxic to aquatic organisms. Bioaccumulation of this chemical may occur in fish.[^2]

### Environmental fate summary

- Dicumyl peroxide's production and use as a polymerization catalyst and vulcanizing agent will result in its release to the environment through various waste streams. If released to air, an estimated vapor pressure of 7.5X10-6 mm Hg at 25 °C indicates dicumyl peroxide will exist in both the vapor and particulate phases in the atmosphere. Vapor-phase dicumyl peroxide will be degraded in the atmosphere by reaction with photochemically-produced hydroxyl radicals; the half-life for this reaction in air is estimated to be 42 hours. Particulate-phase dicumyl peroxide will be removed from the atmosphere by wet and dry deposition. …[^4]

### Persistence and degradability

- TERRESTRIAL FATE: Based on a classification scheme(1), an estimated Koc value of 322,900(SRC), determined from a structure estimation method(2), indicates that dicumyl peroxide is expected to be immobile in soil(SRC). Volatilization of dicumyl peroxide from moist soil surfaces is expected(SRC) given an estimated Henry's Law constant of 4.4X10-5 atm-cu m/mole(SRC), using a fragment constant estimation method(2). However, adsorption to soil is expected to attenuate volatilization(SRC). Dicumyl peroxide is not expected to volatilize from dry soil surfaces(SRC) based upon an estimated vapor pressure of 7.5X10-6 mm Hg at 25 °C(SRC), determined from a fragment constant method(2). …[^4]
- AQUATIC FATE: Based on a classification scheme(1), an estimated Koc value of 322,900(SRC), determined from a structure estimation method(2), indicates that dicumyl peroxide is expected to adsorb to suspended solids and sediment(SRC). Volatilization from [[Water|water]] surfaces is expected(3) based upon an estimated Henry's Law constant of 4.4X10-5 atm-cu m/mole(SRC), developed using a fragment constant estimation method(2). Using this Henry's Law constant and an estimation method(3), volatilization half-lives for a model river and model lake are 37 hours and 16 days, respectively(SRC). …[^4]
- The rate constant for the vapor-phase reaction of dicumyl peroxide with photochemically-produced hydroxyl radicals has been estimated as 9.3X10-12 cu cm/molecule-sec at 25 °C(SRC) using a structure estimation method(1). This corresponds to an atmospheric half-life of about 42 hours at an atmospheric concentration of 5X10+5 hydroxyl radicals per cu cm(1). Dicumyl peroxide has the potential to photolyze directly from natural sunlight based on UV absorption bands that extend beyond 290 nm(2), but the kinetics of potential photolysis are unknown(SRC).[^4]

### Bioaccumulative potential

- BCFs of 181-667 and 137-1470 were measured using carp (Cyprinus carpio) which were exposed over an 8-week period to respective concentrations of 1 and 10 ug/L of dicumyl peroxide(1). According to a classification scheme(2), these BCF ranges suggest bioconcentration in aquatic organisms is high to very high(SRC).[^4]

### Mobility in soil

- Using a structure estimation method based on molecular connectivity indices(1), the Koc of dicumyl peroxide can be estimated to be 322,900(SRC). According to a classification scheme(2), this estimated Koc value suggests that dicumyl peroxide is expected to be immobile in soil(SRC).[^4]
