## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

- GRADUALLY DECOMP LOSING AVAIL OXYGEN, MORE QUICKLY AT HIGHER TEMP, COMPLETELY AT ABOUT 100 °C[^12]

### Hazardous decomposition

- Dangerous when heated to decomp, emits highly toxic fumes of /sulfur oxides/.[^12]

### Chemical dangers

- Heating may cause violent combustion or explosion. Decomposes on heating. This produces toxic fumes including sulfur oxides. The substance is a strong oxidant. It reacts with combustible and reducing materials. The solution in [[Water|water]] is a medium strong acid. Reacts violently with chlorates and perchlorates. This generates explosion hazard. Reacts in the presence of water with metals such as aluminium. This generates fire hazard.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Water soluble. Slowly decomposed by water. The salt rapidly liberates oxygen when heated, and especially so when wet.[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Oxidizing Agents, Strong[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- POTASSIUM PERSULFATE is an oxidizing agent. Noncombustible but accelerates the burning of combustible material. Potassium persulfate plus a little potassium hydroxide and water released sufficient heat and oxygen to ignite a polythene (polyethylene) liner in a container. \[MCA Case History 1155. 1955\].[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- POTASSIUM PERSULFATE PLUS A LITTLE POTASSIUM HYDROXIDE & WATER IGNITED POLYTHENE (POLYETHYLENE) LINER OF CONTAINER BY SIMULTANEOUS RELEASE OF HEAT & OXYGEN.[^12]
- Explosive reaction with chlorates and perchlorates at room temperature in the presence of water; Combustion reaction with metallic dust, such as aluminum, in the presence of moisture; Combustion reaction with sulfur when heated with percussion in the presence of moisture.[^12]
- The material reacts with moisture to form ozone and [[Sulfuric acid|sulfuric acid]] causing explosion in the closed containers.[^12]

## 11. Toxicological Information

### Inhalation risk

- Evaporation at 20 °C is negligible; a harmful concentration of airborne particles can, however, be reached quickly on spraying or when dispersed, especially if powdered.[^2]

### Short-term exposure

- The substance is irritating to the eyes, skin and respiratory tract. Inhalation of dust may cause asthma-like reactions.[^2]

### Long-term exposure

- Repeated or prolonged contact with skin may cause dermatitis. Repeated or prolonged contact may cause skin sensitization. Repeated or prolonged inhalation may cause asthma. May cause a general allergic reaction, such as urticaria or shock.[^2]

### Toxicity summary

- **Cosmetic Ingredient Review Conclusion:** The CIR Expert Panel concluded that Ammonium, Potassium, and [[Sodium persulfate|Sodium Persulfate]] are safe as used as oxidizing agents in hair colorants and hair lighteners designed for brief discontinuous use followed by thorough rinsing from the hair and skin. The Panel also concluded that the available data are insufficient for determining the safety of these persulfates in leave-on products and dentifrices.[^4]

### Routes of exposure

- The substance can be absorbed into the body by inhalation of its aerosol and by ingestion.[^2]

### Signs and symptoms

- **Inhalation Exposure:** Cough. Wheezing. Sore throat. Laboured breathing.[^2]
- **Skin Exposure:** Redness. Pain.[^2]
- **Ingestion Exposure:** Nausea. Vomiting. Abdominal pain. Diarrhoea.[^2]

### Human toxicity

- ONLY ONE INSTANCE OF BURN OF HUMAN CORNEA SEEMS TO HAVE BEEN RECORDED AND THIS HEALED IN 48 HR. NO DETAILS HAVE BEEN FURNISHED.[^12]
- MODERATE IRRITANT & ALLERGEN.[^12]
