## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

- Stable under recommended storage conditions.[^4]

### Hazardous decomposition

- Hazardous decomposition products formed under fire conditions. - Carbon oxides, Hydrogen bromide gas.[^4]
- Readily decomposes when heated to emit toxic fumes of /hydrogen bromide/.[^4]

### Corrosivity

- Liquid ethyl bromide will attack some forms of plastics, rubber and coatings.[^4]

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes on burning. This produces toxic and corrosive gases. Reacts violently with oxidants, strong bases, aluminium, zinc and magnesium. Attacks plastics and rubber.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Highly flammable. Slightly soluble in [[Water|water]] and denser than water. Turns yellow on exposure to air and light.[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Halogenated Organic Compounds[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- ETHYL BROMIDE will react with steam to produce toxic and corrosive fumes. It can react vigorously with oxidizers. It reacts with strong bases. It also reacts with chemically active metals such as sodium, potassium, calcium, powdered aluminum, zinc and magnesium. It will attack some forms of plastics, rubber and coatings. (NTP, 1992)[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Chemically-active metals such as sodium, potassium, calcium, powdered aluminum, zinc & magnesium[^12]
- Incompatible materials: Carbon oxides, Hydrogen bromide gas.[^4]
- The vigorous interaction of ethanol, phosphorus, and bromine to give bromoethane is considered too dangerous for a school experiment.[^4]
- Reacts with water or steam to produce toxic & corrosive fumes. Vigorous reaction with oxidizing materials.[^4]

## 11. Toxicological Information

### Inhalation risk

- A harmful contamination of the air can be reached very quickly on evaporation of this substance at 20 °C.[^2]

### Short-term exposure

- The substance is irritating to the eyes. The substance may cause effects on the central nervous system. Exposure could cause unconsciousness.[^2]

### Toxicity summary

- IDENTIFICATION AND USE: Ethyl bromide is a colorless liquid with a vapor heavier than air. Ethyl bromide is used as an ethylating agent in chemical synthesis. Ethyl bromide was formerly used as a narcotic agent for dental and medical surgery. HUMAN STUDIES: The main health concerns relating to the exposure of ethyl bromide are the potential for neurotoxicity, irritation of the respiratory tract, damage to genetic material, carcinogenicity, hematotoxicity and hepatotoxicity. Limited human experience during surgical anesthesia has indicated that, in addition to CNS depression, there is a possibility of lung congestion and degeneration of the liver and kidney tissues. …[^4]

### Carcinogenicity

- Evaluation: No epidemiological data relevant to the carcinogenicity of bromoethane were available. There is limited evidence in experimental animals for the carcinogenicity of bromoethane. Overall evaluation: Bromoethane is not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans (Group 3).[^4]
- **IARC Carcinogenic Agent:** Bromoethane[^16]
- **IARC Carcinogenic Classes:** Group 3: Not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans[^16]
- **IARC Monographs:** Volume 52: (1991) Chlorinated Drinking-water; Chlorination By-products; Some Other Halogenated Compounds; Cobalt and Cobalt Compounds Volume 71: (1999) Re-evaluation of Some Organic Chemicals, Hydrazine and Hydrogen Peroxide (Part 1, Part 2, Part 3)[^16]

### Routes of exposure

- The substance can be absorbed into the body by inhalation and by ingestion.[^2]
- inhalation, ingestion, skin and/or eye contact[^12]

### Signs and symptoms

- **Inhalation Exposure:** Drowsiness. Unconsciousness.[^2]
- **Eye Exposure:** Pain. Redness.[^2]
- irritation eyes, skin, respiratory system; central nervous system depression; pulmonary edema; liver, kidney disease; cardiac arrhythmias, cardiac arrest[^12]

### Target organs

- Eyes, skin, respiratory system, liver, kidneys, cardiovascular system, central nervous system[^12]

### Acute toxicity values

- LC50 Rat inhalation 27,000 ppm/1 hr[^4]
- LC50 Mouse inhalation 16,230 ppm/1 hr[^4]

### Medical surveillance

- Employees should be screened for history of certain medical conditions which might place the employee at increased risk from ethyl bromide exposure.
