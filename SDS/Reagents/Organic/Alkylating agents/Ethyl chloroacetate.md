## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Hazardous decomposition

- WHEN HEATED TO DECOMP, IT EMITS HIGHLY TOXIC FUMES OF /HYDROGEN CHLORIDE/.[^4]

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes on burning. This produces toxic and corrosive gases including hydrogen chloride and acetic acid fumes. Reacts with [[Water|water]], moist air and acids. This produces hydrogen chloride. Reacts with bases, oxidants and reducing agents.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Highly flammable. Slow hydrolysis to acidic products will cause slow corrosion of common metals. No hazard involved. (USCG, 1999).[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Esters, Sulfate Esters, Phosphate Esters, Thiophosphate Esters, and Borate Esters Halogenated Organic Compounds[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- ETHYL CHLOROACETATE is a chlorinated ester. Esters react with acids to liberate heat along with alcohols and acids. Strong oxidizing acids may cause a vigorous reaction that is sufficiently exothermic to ignite the reaction products. Heat is also generated by the interaction of esters with caustic solutions. Flammable hydrogen is generated by mixing esters with alkali metals and hydrides.[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- ... CAN REACT VIGOROUSLY WITH OXIDIZING MATERIALS. WILL REACT WITH WATER OR STEAM TO PRODUCE TOXIC & CORROSIVE FUMES. VIGOROUS REACTION WITH SODIUM CYANIDE.[^4]
