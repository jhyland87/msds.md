### Description

- Diethylzinc is a pyrophoric liquid with a garlic-like odor. It is stable when it is shipped in sealed tubes with carbon dioxide. It may decompose violently in [[Water|water]] and ignite spontaneously with air. It is toxic by ingestion. If exposed to heat or flame, containers of this material may explode. It is used as an aircraft fuel.[^1]
- Colorless liquid with a garlic-like odor; \[HSDB\] Highly flammable in air and reactive with water; \[Sullivan, p. 979\][^2]

### Recommended use and restrictions

- **Sources/Uses:** Used as a chemical intermediate, a catalyst, an aerospace fuel, and a deacidification agent in paper manufacturing; \[HSDB\][^2]
- **Industrial Processes with risk of exposure:** Pulp and Paper Processing \[Category: Industry\][^2]
- ORGANIC SYNTHESIS; CATALYST FOR POLYMERIZATION OF OLEFINS; HIGH ENERGY AIRCRAFT & MISSILE FUEL; PRODUCTION OF ETHYL MERCURIC CHLORIDE[^3]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

- STABLE IN SEALED TUBE & CARBON DIOXIDE[^3]

### Hazardous decomposition

- When heated to decomp ... emits toxic fumes of /zinc oxide/.[^3]
- ... decomp violently in [[Water|water]].[^3]

### Air and water reactions

- Highly flammable. Ignites in air with a blue flame giving off a peculiar garlic-like odor, \[Merck, 11th ed., 1989\]. Diethyl zinc is spontaneously flammable in air, \[Douda(1966)\]. Reacts violently with water to form flammable ethane gas, \[Brauer(1965)\].[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Metal Hydrides, Metal Alkyls, Metal Aryls, and Silanes[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- DIETHYLZINC is pyrophoric in air, it ignites instantaneously. It reacts explosively with alcohols (methanol, ethanol), bromine, chlorine or liquefied sulfur dioxide \[Houben-Weyl, 1973, 13.2a, p. 855, 757, 709\]. Reaction with water, nitro compounds, arsenic trichloride, phosphorus trichloride is violent \[Bretherick, 5th ed., 1995, p. 587\].[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Reacts violently with bromine, water, nitro compounds. ... Reacts violently with nonmetal halides (e.g., arsenic trichloride or phosphorus trichloride to produce pyrophoric triethyl arsine or triethyl phosphine).[^3]
- Reacts violently /with water/ to form flammable ethane gas.[^3]
- Diethyl zinc and ethyl sodium react with water to produce ethane, a very flammable gas.[^3]
- Ignition occurs on contact with chlorine.[^3]
