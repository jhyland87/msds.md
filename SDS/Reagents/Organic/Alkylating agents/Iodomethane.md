### Description

- Methyl iodide appears as a colorless liquid that turns brown on exposure to light. Denser than [[Water|water]]. Contact may irritate skin, eyes and mucous membranes. Very toxic by ingestion, inhalation and skin absorption.[^1]
- COLOURLESS LIQUID WITH CHARACTERISTIC ODOUR. TURNS BROWN ON EXPOSURE TO LIGHT AND MOISTURE.[^2]

### Recommended use and restrictions

- **Sources/Uses:** Used as a methylating agent; used in microscopy because of its high refractive index; \[ACGIH\][^3]
- Methyl iodide is used as an intermediate in the manufacture of some pharmaceuticals and pesticides. It is also used in methylation processes and in the field of microscopy. Proposed uses of methyl iodide are as a fire extinguisher and as an insecticidal fumigant.[^4]
- Methylating agent; in microscopy because of its high refractive index; as imbedding material for examining diatoms; in testing for pyridine. Light sensitive etching agent for electronic circuits; component in fire extinguishers.[^5]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

### Hazardous decomposition

- IODIDE & HYDROGEN IODIDE MAY BE RELEASED WHEN METHYL IODIDE UNDERGOES THERMAL DECOMPOSITION (270 °C).[^5]

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes above 270 °C . This produces hydrogen iodide. Reacts with strong oxidants. This generates explosion hazard. Reacts with oxygen at 300 °C. This generates explosion hazard.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Soluble in [[Water|water]]. Sinks and slowly decomposes in water forming poisonous vapor cloud of HI.[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Halogenated Organic Compounds[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- Halogenated aliphatic compounds, such as METHYL IODIDE, are moderately or very reactive. Halogenated organics generally become less reactive as more of their hydrogen atoms are replaced with halogen atoms. Low molecular weight haloalkanes are highly flammable and can react with some metals to form dangerous products. Materials in this group are incompatible with strong oxidizing and reducing agents. Also, they are incompatible with many amines, alkylphosphines, nitrides, azo/diazo compounds, alkali metals (sodium), and epoxides.[^1]
