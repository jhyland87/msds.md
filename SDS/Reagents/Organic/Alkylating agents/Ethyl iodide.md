## 4. First-Aid Measures

### First aid by exposure route

- **Inhalation First Aid:** Fresh air, rest. Half-upright position. Artificial respiration may be needed. Refer for medical attention.[^1]
- **Skin First Aid:** Rinse skin with plenty of [[Water|water]] or shower. Refer for medical attention .[^1]
- **Eye First Aid:** First rinse with plenty of water for several minutes (remove contact lenses if easily possible), then refer for medical attention.[^1]
- **Ingestion First Aid:** Rinse mouth. Do NOT induce vomiting. Give one or two glasses of water to drink. Refer for medical attention .[^1]

## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes on burning. This produces iodine and hydrogen iodide. Reacts with strong bases and strong oxidants.[^1]

## 11. Toxicological Information

### Inhalation risk

- No indication can be given about the rate at which a harmful concentration of this substance in the air is reached on evaporation at 20 °C.[^1]

### Short-term exposure

- The substance is irritating to the eyes, skin and respiratory tract. The substance may cause effects on the central nervous system. This may result in lowering of consciousness.[^1]

### Routes of exposure

- The substance can be absorbed into the body by inhalation of its vapour, through the skin and by ingestion.[^1]

### Signs and symptoms

- **Inhalation Exposure:** Confusion. Cough. Drowsiness. Dizziness. Shortness of breath. Sore throat. Unconsciousness.[^1]
- **Skin Exposure:** Redness. Pain.[^1]
- **Ingestion Exposure:** Abdominal pain. Confusion. Dizziness. Drowsiness. Unconsciousness.[^1]
