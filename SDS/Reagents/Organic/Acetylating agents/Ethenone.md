### Description

- Ketene appears as a colorless highly reactive gas with a penetrating odor. mp: -150 °C, bp: -56 °C. Reacts violently with [[Water|water]]. Soluble in diethyl ether and acetone. Severely irritates the eyes, the skin and the respiratory tract. Used is the manufacture of [[Acetic anhydride|acetic anhydride]], sorbic acid, cinnamic acids, chloroacetyl chloride and other materials. Readily polymerizes and cannot be shipped or stored. ## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical dangers

- The substance may readily polymerize. Reacts violently with many organic compounds. Reacts with [[Water|water]]. This produces acetic acid. Decomposes in alcohol and ammonia.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- No rapid reaction with air. Reacts violently with water to form acetic acid.[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Ketones Hydrocarbons, Aliphatic Unsaturated Polymerizable Compounds[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- KETENE is extremely flammable. Mixtures with air are explosive. Reacts violently with water; reacts vigorously with alcohols, ammonia. Readily polymerizes and cannot be shipped or stored. Is obtained instead as needed from diketene. DIKETENE is extremely flammable. Forms explosive vapor/air mixtures above 33 °C. Undergoes a further exothermic polymerization that is retarded by storage in the solid state (below -6.5 °C) or by the use of stabilizing additives. This polymerization becomes violent (with risk of fire or explosion) with warming or on contact with acids or bases. Reacts violently with water.[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Water, alcohols, ammonia \[Note: Readily polymerizes. Reacts with water to form acetic acid.\][^11]
- Interaction /of hydrogen peroxide/ with excess ketene rapidly forms explosive diacetyl peroxide.[^4]

## 11. Toxicological Information

### Inhalation risk

- A harmful concentration of this gas in the air will be reached very quickly on loss of containment.[^2]
