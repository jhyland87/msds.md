### Description

- Acetic anhydride appears as a clear colorless liquid with a strong odor of vinegar. Flash point 129 °F. Corrosive to metals and tissue. Density 9.0 lb /gal. Used to make fibers, plastics, pharmaceuticals, dyes, and explosives.[^1]
- COLOURLESS LIQUID WITH PUNGENT ODOUR.[^2]

### Recommended use and restrictions

- **Sources/Uses:** Used to manufacture cellulose acetate fibers, plastics, pharmaceuticals, dyes, perfumes, explosives, and food starches; \[ACGIH\][^3]

- Manufacture of acetyl compounds, cellulose acetates. As acetulizer and solvent in examining wool fat, glycerol, fatty and volatile oils, resins; detection of rosin. Widely used in organic synthesis, e.g., as dehydrating agent in nitrations, sulfonations and other reactions where removal of [[Water|water]] is necessary. ## 10. Stability and Reactivity

### Chemical stability

- Stable under recommended storage conditions.[^4]
- Volatile[^4]

### Hazardous decomposition

- When heated to decomposition it emits toxic fumes.[^4]
- Decomposes slowly in [[Water|water]] to form acetic acid.[^4]

### Corrosivity

- Corrosive[^4]

### Polymerization

- Polymerization does not occur.[^4]

### Chemical dangers

- Decomposes on burning. This produces carbon monoxide (see ICSC 0023) and toxic fumes including acetic acid fumes. Reacts violently with strong reducing agents, oxidants, strong bases and water. This generates fire and explosion hazard. Attacks many metals and plastics.[^2]

### Air and water reactions

- Flammable. Reacts violently with water to generate acetic acid. This reaction is heightened by the presence of mineral acids (nitric, perchloric, [[Sulfuric acid|sulfuric acid]], etc.) \[Chem. Eng. News 25, 3458\].[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Anhydrides[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- ACETIC ANHYDRIDE reacts violently on contact with water, steam, methanol, ethanol, glycerol and boric acid. Reaction with water is particularly dangerous in presence with mineral acids (e.g., nitric, perchloric, chromic, sulfuric acid) \[Chem. Eng. News 25, 3458\]. Potentially explosive reactions with oxidizing reagents such as barium peroxide, chromium trioxide, chromic acid, hypochlorous acid, [[Nitric acid (azeotropic, 68%)|nitric acid]], perchloric acid, peroxyacetic acid, [[Potassium permanganate|potassium permanganate]], hydrogen peroxide. \[Sax, 9th ed., 1996, p. 15\]. Reacts violently with metal nitrates used as nitrating agents \[Davey W. et al., Chem. & Ind., 1948, p. 814\].[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- Water, alcohols, strong oxidizers (especially chromic acid), amines, strong caustics \[Note: Corrosive to iron, steel & other metals. Reacts with water to form acetic acid.\][^12]
