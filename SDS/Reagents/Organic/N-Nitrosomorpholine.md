No information is available on the acute (short-term), chronic (long-term), reproductive, developmental, or carcinogenic effects of N-nitrosomorpholine in humans. Animal studies have reported effects on the liver from chronic exposure as well as tumors of the liver, nasal cavity, lung, and kidneys from oral exposure to N-nitrosomorpholine. EPA has not classified N- nitrosomorpholine for carcinogenicity. The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) has classified N-nitrosomorpholine as a Group 2B, possible human carcinogen.[^4]

### Environmental fate summary

- N-Nitrosomorpholine may be released to the environment in emissions and effluents from facilities which produce rubber and related products. This compound may also be released to the environment as a result of nitrosation of morpholine. High concentrations of acids, nitrates, and nitrites in process streams provide excellent conditions for nitrosation to occur. N-Nitrosomorpholine has been detected as a by-product of surface [[Water|water]] disinfection treatment processes such as chlorination. If released to air, N-nitrosomorpholine's estimated vapor pressure of 0.036 mm Hg at 20 °C indicates this compound will exist solely as a vapor in the ambient atmosphere. …[^5]

### Persistence and degradability

- TERRESTRIAL FATE: Based on a classification scheme(1), estimated Koc values of 22 and 4(SRC), determined respectively from a structure estimation method(2) and using a log Kow of -0.44(3) and a regression-derived equation(2), indicates that N-nitrosomorpholine is expected to have very high mobility in soil(SRC). Although N-nitrosomorpholine's estimated vapor pressure of 0.036 mm Hg at 20 °C(4) and measured Henry's Law constant of 2.45X10-8 atm-cu m/mole(5) suggest that volatilization from dry and moist soil is not expected to occur(SRC), inhalation toxicity studies found that N-nitrosomorpholine was volatile at room temperatures with inhalation exposure possible(6). …[^5]
- AQUATIC FATE: Based on a classification scheme(1), estimated Koc values of 22 and 6(SRC), determined respectively from a structure estimation method(2) and using a log Kow of -0.44(3) and a regression-derived equation(2), indicate that N-nitrosomorpholine is not expected to adsorb to suspended solids and sediment(SRC). N-Nitrosomorpholine is not expected to volatilize from water surfaces(4) based on a measured Henry's Law constant of 2.45X10-8 atm-cu m/mole(5). According to a classification scheme(6), an estimated BCF of 3(SRC), from a its log Kow and a regression-derived equation(2), suggests the potential for bioconcentration in aquatic organisms is low. …[^5]
- The rate constant for the vapor-phase reaction of N-nitrosomorpholine with photochemically-produced hydroxyl radicals has been estimated as 7.48X10-11 cu cm/molecule-sec at 25 °C(SRC) using a structure estimation method(1). This corresponds to an atmospheric half-life of about 5 hours at an atmospheric concentration of 5X10+5 hydroxyl radicals per cu cm(1). N-Nitrosomorpholine in water has a UV absorption maximum at 346 nm(2), indicating that it has the potential to undergo direct photolysis in air, water, and on soil surfaces(SRC). Nitrosamines photolyze rapidly in sunlight in aqueous solution and in the gas phase forming an amino radical and NO(2-4). …[^5]
