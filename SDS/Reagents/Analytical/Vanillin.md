### Air and water reactions

- Slowly oxidizes on exposure to air. (NTP, 1992). Slightly [[Water|water]] soluble.[^1]

### Reactive groups

- Aldehydes Ethers Phenols and Cresols[^1]

### Reactivity profile

- VANILLIN can react violently with Br2, HClO4, potassium-tert-butoxide, (tert-chloro-benzene + NaOH), (formic acid + Tl(NO3)3). (NTP, 1992). VANILLIN is an aldehyde. Aldehydes are readily oxidized to give carboxylic acids. Flammable and/or toxic gases are generated by the combination of aldehydes with azo, diazo compounds, dithiocarbamates, nitrides, and strong reducing agents. Aldehydes can react with air to give first peroxo acids, and ultimately carboxylic acids. These autoxidation reactions are activated by light, catalyzed by salts of transition metals, and are autocatalytic (catalyzed by the products of the reaction).[^1]

### Hazardous reactions and incompatibilities

- A VIOLENT REACTION OCCURRED WHEN SMALL AMT OF VANILLIN WAS ADDED TO THALLIUM TRINITRATE TRIHYDRATE (UP TO 50%) IN 90% FORMIC ACID.[^4]
- Can react violently with Br2; HClO4; potassium-tert-butoxide; tert-chlorobenzene + NaOH; formic acid + thallium nitrate.[^4]

### Signs and symptoms

- **Eye Exposure:** Redness.[^2]

### Acute toxicity values

- LD50 Rat oral 1580 mg/kg[^4]
- LD50 Rat ip 1160 mg/kg[^4]
- LD50 Rat sc 1500 mg/kg[^4]
- LD50 Mouse ip 475 mg/kg[^4]

### Human toxicity

- /HUMAN EXPOSURE STUDIES/ In closed-patch tests on human skin vanillin caused no primary irritation when tested at concentrations of 20% on 29 normal subjects, at 2% on 30 normal subjects, and at 0.4% on 35 subjects with dermatoses. Maximization tests were conducted on groups of 25 volunteers. The material was tested at concentrations of 2 and 5% in petrolatum and produced no sensitization reactions.[^4]
- /HUMAN EXPOSURE STUDIES/ Vanillin applied undiluted for 48 hr in the standard occluded aluminum-patch test used by the North American Dermatitis Research Group did not produce any irritation or sensitization in a 62-yr-old subject with a perfume dermatitis.[^4]

### Populations at special risk

- Positive reactions to vanillin were reported in 8 out of 142 patients who were already sensitized to balsam of Peru. In studies of sensitization to balsam of Peru and its components, vanillin, pure or 10% in vaseline, produced positive patch-test reactions in 21 out of 164 patients sensitive to balsam. Vanillin was considered to be a secondary allergen because sensitivity was found only in patients sensitive to vanilla, isoeugenol, and coniferyl benzoate. Cross-sensitization to other benzaldehydes was particularly uncommon. Vanillin was found not to be responsible for most cases of sensitivity to natural vanilla.[^4]
